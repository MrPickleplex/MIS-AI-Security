Malware and Cyber Attacks

ILOVEYOU

ILOVEYOU was a computer worm released on May 4th, 2000 that spread through email attachments. It is an example of social engineering being used to aid its spread, as it disguised as a love letter to the recipient. It is estimated that 1 in every 10 internet-connected computers worldwide at the time was infected with this virus, making it one of the most virulent malware attacks in history; the damages cause by this worm were measured to be around $10 billion USD. Its danger came from the sheer volume of incoming mail this worm would produce, which could eventually overwrite existing files in the computer with its own source code, leading to important documents such as financial data to be lost. This is what contributed to the damages this worm caused. The worm was created by Onel de Guzman in the Philippines, who believed internet access was a human right. To fulfill this aim, ILOVEYOU stole dial-up internet access credentials. Because of the lack of Philippine laws against hacking, his charges were dropped.
